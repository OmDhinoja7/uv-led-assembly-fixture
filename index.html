The moduleUVM-100

A UV LED shines through a plano-convex lens. The lens has to sit at the right height above the LED and on its axis, or the beam goes off target. No adhesive is used: a pressed ring holds the lens.

Tolerance stack-upECO-001 · Rev A → B

The first drawing (Rev A) met neither optical requirement at worst case. I ran both stacks, found the cause of each, and changed the drawings through ECO-001.

StackRev ARev BLimitWhat changed
R1 seat depth, worst case±0.20 fail±0.10 pass±0.15Ledge dimensioned directly from the floor instead of from the bottom face. One link left in the chain instead of two.
R2 lens concentricity, worst case radial0.300 fail0.095 pass0.100Counterbore Ø22.05 +0.02/0 and lens Ø22.00 +0/−0.02, down from general tolerance. Lens float cut from 0.250 to 0.045.

R1 also passed by RSS at Rev A (±0.14). I designed to worst case anyway: R1 is critical to the optics, and an RSS pass still allows a small share of modules out of spec.

Error-proofingR3 · poka-yoke

A rotated LED board still looks fine from above, so visual inspection is a weak control. I made the wrong orientation physically impossible instead: a key tab in the housing engages a notch in the board, and the board only drops flat in one position.

Correct orientation: no interferenceRotated: 4.43 mm³ interference at the tab

Interference detection in SolidWorks confirmed both cases. In the PFMEA this took the board-rotation failure mode to occurrence 1, RPN 18.

ToolingFIX-100 · GAG-100

The nest holds the housing open end up and stable through OP30 to OP50. The gauge body sits on the ledge and the pin rests on the floor. If the pin top lands inside the Ø8 step in the gauge face, the seat depth is in tolerance.

One module down the line

The operator's view, from WI-001. Each step has a written accept criterion, and the PFMEA and control plan point to those criteria by name.

  1. OP10

    Check the seat depth

    Set GAG-100 on the housing ledge, pin on the floor. Feel where the pin top lands.

    Accept pin top inside the step. A bad housing never reaches the line.

  2. OP20

    Load the nest

    Drop the housing into FIX-100, open end up. The chamfer guides it in.

    Accept flat on the pocket floor, about 5 mm proud.

  3. OP30

    Drop in the LED board

    Line the notch up with the key tab and lower the board flat. Never force it.

    Error-proofed rotated, it will not seat. The tab blocks it.

  4. OP40

    Place the lens, dome up

    Blow off the ledge, pick the lens with the vacuum pen, set it flat side down.

    Accept rim flush and level; no scratches, prints or particles under the light.

  5. OP50

    Press the retainer

    Set the ring on the lens rim and press evenly by hand until it seats.

    Accept flush on the rim, no gap; lens not cracked.

  6. OP60

    Final inspection

    Compare the module with the boundary samples. A power-on light check is planned.

    Accept matches the samples and every earlier criterion.

Validation planningVP-001 · MSA-001 · ECO-002

VP-001 is the IQ/OQ/PQ protocol for the nest: 10 installation checks against the drawing, 6 operational tests at the worst-case part limits, and a performance run of 3 × 20 modules across operators and shifts. 59 straight passes demonstrate 95% reliability at 95% confidence (success-run theorem), so 60 with zero failures meets it.

MSA-001 plans an attribute agreement study for the gauge: 50 housings, about half of them near the limits, 3 appraisers, 3 trials each, judged on miss rate first.

Writing these two plans caught two problems that a design review had missed.

  1. 2026-10-19
    ECO-001, housing Rev B. Seat depth dimensioned from the floor, counterbore and lens tolerances tightened. R1 and R2 pass at worst case.
  2. 2026-11-06
    ECO-002, housing Rev C. The VP-001 fit check showed the housing OD was only covered by ISO 2768-m (±0.2), so a large housing would jam a tight nest pocket by 0.05 mm. Added Ø28.00 ±0.05. Clearance is now 0.10 to 0.30 mm at every combination of limits.
  3. 2026-11-07
    ECO-003, gauge Rev B (open). MSA-001 planning showed the gauge accepts 5.00 ±0.15, sized to the R1 limit instead of the ±0.10 part tolerance, so it would pass some out-of-spec housings. The fix is a 10.10 mm body and a 0.20 mm step. The study waits for this change.
